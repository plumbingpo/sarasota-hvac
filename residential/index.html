Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we touch a tool, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your system's age and tonnage, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

Many HVAC problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Refrigerant seeps out through pinhole leaks in the evaporator coil, blower bearings wear slowly, and a weakening capacitor gives no warning until the compressor won't start on the first hot day. If you notice weak airflow, rising energy bills, ice on the refrigerant lines, or rooms that never reach the thermostat setting, call for an inspection sooner rather than later. Catching it early often keeps a repairable issue from becoming a replacement.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of Sarasota homeowners ask us to size up an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new unit during a cold snap. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Maintenance Plans

Seasonal HVAC Maintenance Plans

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep your home comfortable year-round. Regular service heads off failures before they start, keeps coils and burners clean, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Sarasota HVAC, every plan is built around your home's specific equipment, with scheduled visits that cover coil cleaning, refrigerant charge verification, capacitor and contactor testing, burner and heat exchanger inspection, condensate drain clearing, filter replacement, and a static pressure check across the duct system.

Many homes do best with two visits a year: a springtime tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household works it, and local conditions like dust and pollen, which clog condenser coils and push up energy use quickly.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already bought. Each tune-up keeps the coils clean, the charge correct, and the filter fresh, which lowers what you pay to run the system while easing the strain on the compressor and blower. For most households, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Sized for Your Home

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

When it comes to HVAC, bigger is not better. An oversized unit turns on and off too often, burns extra energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs constantly and still can't keep up on the hottest days. That's why we never just copy the tonnage of the old unit. We run a proper load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

Choosing new equipment is about more than a SEER2 number. Our technician will compare heat pumps against a furnace-and-AC pair, and ducted against ductless, walking through operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Growing families frequently do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds tighter temperatures across the whole house and uses less energy. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Sarasota

No cooling, a furnace that refuses to light, a frozen evaporator coil, a slow refrigerant leak: every one of these problems has its own cause and needs its own repair. Sarasota HVAC tracks down the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's a condenser that hums without starting, a short-cycling furnace, a thermostat that keeps losing its program, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split flashing error codes.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A bad capacitor only keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can let combustion gases into the house, and a blocked condensate drain can soak through a ceiling before anyone notices. Our technicians carry the parts to replace capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, seal ductwork that's leaking air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak has been repaired.
